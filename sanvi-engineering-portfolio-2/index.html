SEC. 01 — ABOUT

Building things and figuring out why they work.

I'm drawn to the point where hardware and software meet — where a line of code has to move a motor, light a display, or make a decision based on what a sensor reports. Most of my projects start on a breadboard or in a lab notebook before they end up working the way I intended, and usually get rebuilt once or twice along the way.

I've worked with digital logic, embedded programming, circuit prototyping, and CAD design through coursework and independent projects, and I'm currently applying that toward engineering internships and research opportunities where I can keep building and asking how systems are put together.

SEC. 03 — METHOD

How I work through a build

The same six-step loop shows up in every notebook, whether the project is a logic circuit or a physical prototype.

01

Define the problem

Pin down what the project needs to do and what constraints it has to work within.

02

Research

Look at existing approaches and components before committing to a design.

03

Design

Sketch and spec the system — logic, circuit, or CAD — before building it.

04

Build

Breadboard, solder, or assemble the prototype from the design.

05

Test

Run it, measure what actually happens, and compare against the goal.

06

Iterate

Fix what failed, adjust the design, and rebuild until it holds up.
